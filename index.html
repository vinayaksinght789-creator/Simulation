How it works

The inverter U1A is biased into its linear region by Rf. The crystal Y1 acts as a very high-Q inductor, and C1 and C2 complete a π-network that gives the 180° phase shift needed for oscillation. Noise grows each cycle until the inverter saturates, so the swing settles near the supply rails.

U1B squares up the sine wave into a clean clock, and the flip-flop halves it. The oscillation frequency is fs·(1 + Cm / 2(C0 + CL)), with CL = C1·C2/(C1+C2) + 3 pF stray. The start-up view compresses time: real crystals take milliseconds (MHz parts) to about a second (32.768 kHz).
